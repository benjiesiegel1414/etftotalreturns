$JEPI vs $SCHD: Total Return, Dividends and Which Is Better in 2026

By Benjamin Siegel · October 31, 2026 · 9 min read

Sponsored By IncomeBlastETFs.com

On total return, $SCHD has beaten $JEPI: 10.61% a year over the last 5 years versus 7.44%, and 38.50% versus 9.56% over the past year, per StockAnalysis data through October 30, 2026. $JEPI's edge is income and a calmer ride: a 7.93% yield paid monthly versus 2.95% paid quarterly for $SCHD. Which one is "better" comes down to whether you need the cash today or the compounding later.

"$JEPI vs $SCHD" is one of the most searched matchups that lands on this site. People type it every way: "jepi or schd", "schd vs jepi", "jepi vs schd total return", "how does jepi compare with schd". And it makes sense. These are two of the most popular income ETFs on the planet, and they get pitched as the same thing even though they're built completely differently.

One is a dividend growth fund. The other is an options income fund. So let's put them side by side on the one number that settles it: total return.

On $10,000 over the last 5 years, that works out to roughly $16,550 for $SCHD versus $14,320 for $JEPI. For reference, $VOO turned the same $10,000 into about $17,660.

The last 12 months are even more lopsided. $10,000 in $SCHD a year ago is about $13,850 today. In $JEPI, it's about $10,960. That's a value and dividend stock rally, and $SCHD was right in the middle of it.

Here's the honest part, though. One great year doesn't make $SCHD a 38% a year fund, and it wasn't long ago that $SCHD was the laggard everyone complained about. That's exactly why I look at the 5 year number first. Over that full window, which includes the 2022 bear market, $SCHD still came out ahead by about 3 percentage points a year.

What's the difference between $JEPI and $SCHD?

This is the part most people miss. These two funds don't just hold different stocks. They make money in completely different ways.

$SCHD: the dividend growth fund

$SCHD, the Schwab U.S. Dividend Equity ETF, tracks the Dow Jones U.S. Dividend 100 Index. It screens for companies with a long record of paying dividends plus strong cash flow, return on equity and dividend yield, then holds about 100 of them. Top names right now include Texas Instruments, Chevron, ConocoPhillips, Qualcomm, Procter & Gamble and Coca-Cola.

Your return comes from two places: the dividends those companies pay, and the stock prices going up over time. There's no options overlay. When the stocks rally, you get all of it.

$JEPI: the options income fund

$JEPI, the JPMorgan Equity Premium Income ETF, is actively managed. It owns a portfolio of lower volatility large cap U.S. stocks, then adds equity linked notes (ELNs) that sell call options on the S&P 500. Those options throw off premium, and that premium is most of the monthly paycheck.

The trade off is simple. Selling calls caps your upside. When the market rips higher, $JEPI gives a chunk of that gain away in exchange for the income it already collected. That's why its beta is just 0.52 versus 0.68 for $SCHD and about 1.0 for $VOO. Calmer ride, smaller upside.

$JEPI vs $SCHD dividends: which pays more?

$JEPI, by a lot, at least on the check you see each month.

  • $JEPI paid $4.56 per share over the last 12 months, a 7.93% yield. Recent monthly payments ranged from $0.34134 (October 2026) to $0.44761 (May 2026).
  • $SCHD paid $1.05 per share over the last 12 months, a 2.95% yield. The last four quarterly payments were $0.2782, $0.2569, $0.2525 and $0.2665.

On a $100,000 position, that's roughly $7,930 a year (about $660 a month) from $JEPI versus $2,950 a year from $SCHD. If you're retired and covering bills, that gap is real money, and I'd never tell someone it doesn't matter.

But look at the direction. $JEPI's payout moves around month to month because it depends on option premium, and it was down 3.87% over the last year. $SCHD's dividend is smaller but it comes from company profits, and it grew 1.95% over the last year. When I think about income, I care about where the check is going, not just how big it is today.

Why the higher yield didn't win

This is the whole reason this site exists. Yield is not return.

$JEPI paid out more than double the yield, but $SCHD still won on total return over both the 1 year and 5 year windows. Why? Because $JEPI's price barely grows. It trades around the high $50s, and it's been in a tight range for a long time. Its 52 week range is $55.10 to $59.90. Almost all of its return is the distribution.

$SCHD's share price, on the other hand, went from a 52 week low of $26.32 to about $35.78. That price growth, on top of a smaller dividend, is what crushed $JEPI over the last year.

I'm not saying $JEPI is bad. It's doing exactly what it's designed to do: pay you a lot now and smooth out the ride. Just don't confuse a big monthly check with a better investment. The tax angle

This one matters if you hold these in a regular brokerage account.

Most of $SCHD's dividends are generally qualified dividends, which are taxed at lower long term capital gains rates. Most of $JEPI's distributions come from the option premium inside the ELNs, which is generally taxed as ordinary income. That means in a taxable account, $JEPI's 7.93% shrinks more after taxes than $SCHD's 2.95% does.

A lot of income investors handle this by holding funds like $JEPI in an IRA and funds like $SCHD in taxable. Your situation is your own, so check with a tax professional.

How I'd actually think about $JEPI vs $SCHD

Here's what I actually look at: what job does this money have?

  • If you need the income now (retired, covering bills, living off the portfolio), $JEPI's monthly check does something $SCHD can't. You're trading future growth for present cash flow, and that can be a perfectly reasonable trade.
  • If you're still building and reinvesting, $SCHD has been the better compounder, with a lower fee, a growing dividend and full upside when the market runs.
  • If you want both, plenty of people own the two together. $SCHD is the growing base, $JEPI is the income booster. Just know you're paying 0.35% on the $JEPI side versus 0.06% on $SCHD. On $100,000, that's $350 a year versus $60.

Personally, I'd rather own a dividend that grows than chase a yield that slowly drifts. That's how I've always thought about building income. But I also understand why someone a few years from retirement would want $JEPI's paycheck. There's no wrong answer if you know what you're giving up.

The risks

  • $JEPI caps your upside. In strong bull markets it will lag, sometimes badly. You won't get that back later.
  • $JEPI's payout isn't fixed. It rises and falls with option premiums and market volatility. Calm markets can mean smaller checks.
  • $JEPI uses ELNs. Those are notes issued by banks, so there's counterparty exposure on top of market risk.
  • $SCHD is concentrated in value sectors. Energy, consumer staples and health care names carry a lot of weight. When growth and tech lead, $SCHD can trail the S&P 500 for years.
  • Recent returns are not a forecast. $SCHD's 38.50% year was unusual. Don't buy any fund because of its last 12 months.

The takeaway

On total return, $SCHD has been the better fund: 10.61% a year over 5 years versus 7.44% for $JEPI, with a lower fee and a growing dividend. $JEPI wins on income, paying about 7.93% a year in monthly checks with a smoother ride. If you need cash flow now, $JEPI earns its spot. If you're building wealth, $SCHD has done the heavy lifting. Either way, judge them on total return, not yield.

FAQ

Is $JEPI or $SCHD better?
On total return, $SCHD has been better. Through October 30, 2026, StockAnalysis shows $SCHD up 10.61% a year over 5 years versus 7.44% for $JEPI, and 38.50% versus 9.56% over the past year. $JEPI pays a much higher monthly income. This is not a recommendation.
How does $JEPI compare with $SCHD?
$SCHD is a dividend growth index fund holding about 100 U.S. dividend stocks, charging 0.06% and paying quarterly. $JEPI is an actively managed fund that sells S&P 500 call options through equity linked notes, charging 0.35% and paying monthly. $JEPI yields more; $SCHD has grown more.
Which pays more, $JEPI or $SCHD?
$JEPI. It paid $4.56 per share over the last 12 months, a 7.93% yield, versus $1.05 per share and a 2.95% yield for $SCHD, per StockAnalysis as of late October 2026. $SCHD's dividend grew 1.95% over the last year while $JEPI's fell 3.87%.
What is the total return of $JEPI vs $SCHD?
Through October 30, 2026: $JEPI 9.56% over 1 year, 7.44% a year over 5 years and 11.24% a year since its May 2020 launch. $SCHD 38.50% over 1 year, 10.61% a year over 5 years and 13.72% a year since its October 2011 launch. All include reinvested dividends.
Should I own both $JEPI and $SCHD?
Some income investors do, using $SCHD as a growing dividend base and $JEPI to boost monthly income. They hold very different strategies, so they can complement each other. Consider fees, taxes and your own income needs, and do your own research.
