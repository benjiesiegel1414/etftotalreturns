$SCHD vs $VYM: Total Return, Dividends and Which Is Better in 2026

By Benjie · November 10, 2026 · 10 min read

Sponsored By IncomeBlastETFs.com

Over 10 years, $SCHD has the better total return: 13.70% a year at NAV versus 12.03% for $VYM, through October 31, 2026. That turns $10,000 into about $36,100 versus $31,100. But it's not a clean sweep. $VYM won over 5 years (11.76% vs 10.62% a year), the two basically tied over 3 years, and $SCHD crushed it over the last 12 months (38.54% vs 19.37%). $SCHD also pays more income today, about 3.2% versus 2.3%.

"$SCHD vs $VYM" is one of the most searched matchups that lands people on this site, along with "$VYM vs $SCHD" and "$VYM stock vs $SCHD". Makes sense. They're the two giant, dirt cheap dividend ETFs, and a lot of people own one and wonder if they picked the wrong one.

So let's settle it the only way this site cares about. Total return: price change plus every dividend reinvested. Straight from Schwab and Vanguard.

Here's what jumps out at me. This matchup flips depending on the window you pick. $SCHD looked dead in the water for a couple of years, and plenty of people dumped it for $VYM or something else. Then 2026 happened, and $SCHD is up more than 33% year to date. Over the full decade, $SCHD is ahead by about $5,000 per $10,000 invested.

This is the part most people miss: if you'd judged these two funds on a 3 or 5 year chart in early 2026, you'd have picked the "wrong" one for the year that followed. Neither fund wins every period. That's exactly why I look at long windows and not last year's chart.

What's the difference between $SCHD and $VYM?

They both say "dividend" on the label, but they're built in completely different ways.

$SCHD tracks the Dow Jones U.S. Dividend 100 Index. It screens for companies with at least 10 straight years of dividends, then scores them on cash flow to debt, return on equity, dividend yield and 5 year dividend growth. The top 100 make it in. It's a quality screen as much as a yield screen.

$VYM tracks the FTSE High Dividend Yield Index. It basically takes the higher yielding half of the U.S. market (excluding REITs) and weights it by market cap. No quality screen, no growth screen. Just yield and size.

What's actually inside each fund

This is where the return gap comes from.

$SCHD's biggest positions are roughly equal weight at the top: Procter & Gamble (4.43%), Texas Instruments (4.31%), Coca-Cola (4.29%), Home Depot (4.27%), Amgen (4.12%), PepsiCo, Chevron, Abbott, ConocoPhillips and UnitedHealth, all around 4%. By sector it's heavy in consumer staples (about 20%), health care (19%) and energy (15%), with only about 9% in financials.

$VYM looks a lot more like the broad market. Broadcom is its top holding at 6.93%, then JPMorgan (3.75%), ExxonMobil (2.84%), Johnson & Johnson (2.71%) and AbbVie (1.96%). Financials are its biggest sector at about 20%, then technology at about 14%. Vanguard uses a different sector scheme than Schwab, so treat the sector numbers as a rough guide, not an exact match.

So the short version: $SCHD is a concentrated bet on about 100 steady, cash rich dividend payers. $VYM is a broad, value tilted slice of 590+ stocks that happens to own big banks and Broadcom. When banks and chip stocks run, $VYM wins. When staples, health care and energy run, $SCHD wins. In 2026 it's been $SCHD's year.

$SCHD vs $VYM dividends: yield and dividend growth

On income today, $SCHD pays more. Schwab lists a 3.24% trailing distribution yield and a 3.14% SEC yield. $VYM paid $3.6755 a share over its last four payments, about 2.26% on its $162.67 NAV (November 9), with a 2.23% SEC yield. On $100,000 that's roughly $3,200 a year from $SCHD versus $2,300 from $VYM.

$SCHD grew its payout about 18% in two years. $VYM's was flat. That's the dividend growth screen doing its job. In 2026 the gap has narrowed: $SCHD's first three payments are up less than 1% from the same three in 2025, while $VYM's are up about 7%. One year doesn't make a trend, but it's worth knowing.

Don't let the per-share numbers fool you. $VYM pays $3.68 a share and $SCHD pays $1.05, but $SCHD's share price is a fraction of $VYM's after the split. Yield is the number that matters.

Fees: basically a tie

$VYM costs 0.04% a year and $SCHD costs 0.06%. On a $100,000 position that's $40 versus $60 a year. A $20 difference. Both are about as cheap as dividend ETFs get, and every return number above is already after fees. I would never pick between these two on cost.

Taxes

Both funds hold U.S. stocks, so their dividends are generally qualified and taxed at the lower long term capital gains rates if you meet the holding period. Neither one has handed out big capital gains distributions. In a taxable account, they're about equally efficient. $SCHD's higher yield does mean a slightly bigger tax bill each year in a taxable account, which matters more if you're still building and reinvesting.

How I'd actually think about $SCHD vs $VYM

I've been a dividend investor for more than 10 years, and I've watched this exact debate swing back and forth. Here's how I break it down:

  • If you want income now and a growing paycheck: $SCHD. Higher yield, and the payout has grown much faster over the last few years.
  • If you want diversification and less single stock risk: $VYM. With 590+ holdings and nothing like a 4% equal weight cluster at the top, it's closer to "own the dividend market".
  • If you can't decide: owning both is reasonable. They overlap on names like Procter & Gamble, Coca-Cola, Home Depot and Chevron, but the weights and the rest of the portfolios are very different.

What I'd avoid is selling one to buy the other because of the last 12 months. People who jumped from $SCHD to $VYM in 2024 and 2025 missed $SCHD's big 2026. The next swing could go the other way.

The risks on each side

$SCHD: concentration. About 41% in the top 10 and big sector bets on staples, health care and energy. It reconstitutes once a year, and its turnover is much higher than $VYM's. Its tech exposure is light, so it will lag in a tech led market, like it did in 2023 and 2024.

$VYM: no quality or growth screen, so it can hold companies with stretched payouts. Its biggest single holding, Broadcom, is nearly 7%, which is a lot of chip exposure for a "high dividend" fund. And its dividend has barely grown in recent years.

The takeaway

Over 10 years through October 31, 2026, $SCHD has the better total return (13.70% vs 12.03% a year), and it pays a higher, faster growing dividend. $VYM won the 5 year window, tied over 3 years, costs a hair less and is far more diversified. If I had to own just one for a growing dividend stream, the record favors $SCHD. If I wanted the broadest, simplest dividend exposure, $VYM does that job well. Either way, read both prospectuses and do your own homework before you buy.

FAQ

Is $SCHD better than $VYM?
Over 10 years, $SCHD has the better total return: 13.70% a year at NAV versus 12.03% for $VYM through October 31, 2026. $VYM won over 5 years (11.76% vs 10.62% a year) and the two were nearly tied over 3 years. $SCHD pays a higher yield and has grown its dividend faster, while $VYM is cheaper and much more diversified. Which is better depends on what you want the fund to do.
What is the total return of $SCHD vs $VYM?
Through October 31, 2026 at NAV, $SCHD returned 38.54% over 1 year, 20.72% a year over 3 years, 10.62% over 5 years and 13.70% over 10 years. $VYM returned 19.37%, 20.93%, 11.76% and 12.03% over the same periods. $10,000 in $SCHD ten years ago grew to about $36,100, versus about $31,100 in $VYM.
Which pays a higher dividend, $SCHD or $VYM?
$SCHD. Its 30 day SEC yield is 3.14% and its trailing distribution yield is 3.24%, versus a 2.23% SEC yield and about 2.3% trailing yield for $VYM. $SCHD's annual dividend per share grew about 18% from 2023 to 2025, while $VYM's was nearly flat.
Do $SCHD and $VYM overlap?
Partly. Both own large dividend payers like Procter & Gamble, Coca-Cola, Home Depot, Chevron and UnitedHealth. But $SCHD holds about 100 stocks with roughly 41% in its top 10, while $VYM holds about 590 stocks with around 26% in its top 10 and much more in banks and Broadcom.
Should I own both $SCHD and $VYM?
Some investors do, since the two funds are built differently and lead in different markets. Both are cheap, at 0.06% and 0.04% a year. Owning both gives you a quality and dividend growth screen plus broad high yield exposure, at the cost of some overlap. Not financial advice.
