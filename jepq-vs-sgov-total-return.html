$JEPQ vs $SGOV: Total Return, Income and Which Is Better in 2026

By Benjie · November 21, 2026 · 10 min read

Sponsored By IncomeBlastETFs.com

Over the last 3 years, $JEPQ has crushed $SGOV on total return: 20.04% a year at NAV through October 31, 2026, versus 4.54% a year for $SGOV through September 30, 2026. But they're built for completely different jobs. $JEPQ is a Nasdaq-100 stock fund that sells calls for an 11% to 12% yield and can drop hard. $SGOV holds 0 to 3 month Treasury bills, yields about 3.6% to 3.9%, and barely moves.

"$JEPQ vs $SGOV," "$SGOV vs $JEPQ" and "$JEPI vs $SGOV" keep showing up in the searches that bring people to this site. I get it. Both pay monthly. Both get bought by people who want income. And with $JEPQ yielding more than three times what $SGOV pays, the question is obvious: why would anyone hold T-bills?

This month is a pretty good answer. Let's walk through it the way this site always does: total return first, then income, risk and fit. Numbers come straight from J.P. Morgan Asset Management and iShares.

What's the difference between $JEPQ and $SGOV?

$JEPQ is the JPMorgan Nasdaq Equity Premium Income ETF, launched May 3, 2022. It owns a portfolio of mostly Nasdaq-100 stocks picked by J.P. Morgan's team, then sells out-of-the-money Nasdaq-100 call options (through equity linked notes) to generate monthly income. J.P. Morgan describes the goal as monthly distributable income and Nasdaq-100 exposure with less volatility. The trade-off: when the Nasdaq rips higher, the calls cap part of the upside.

$SGOV is the iShares 0-3 Month Treasury Bond ETF, launched May 26, 2020. It tracks the ICE 0-3 Month US Treasury Securities Index, which means it holds U.S. Treasury bills that mature within three months. iShares lists an effective duration of 0.11 years and a 3 year standard deviation of 0.21%. In plain English: it's about as close to cash as an ETF gets.

Income: about 12% that moves vs about 3.6% that's steady

On $100,000, here's the rough math today:

  • $JEPQ: about $11,900 a year at J.P. Morgan's 11.87% 12 month rolling yield, paid monthly. The last 12 payments added up to about $6.95 a share.
  • $SGOV: about $3,600 a year at its 3.62% trailing yield, or closer to $3,900 at the 3.89% SEC yield, paid monthly.

Over the last 12 months, $JEPQ's monthly payment ranged from about $0.47 to $0.70 a share. That's a 50% swing from low to high. It moves with option premiums, and option premiums rise when markets get nervous. $SGOV's payment moves too, but only with short-term interest rates, and much more gently.

Here's how I actually think about it. If I need a bill paid next month no matter what, I don't want that money riding on the Nasdaq. If I'm building income over 10 years and can stomach the swings, $JEPQ's extra yield and growth potential are hard to ignore.

Taxes matter here too. $JEPQ's distributions come largely from option premium income through the notes, which is generally taxed as ordinary income in a taxable account. $SGOV's distributions are Treasury bill interest, which is also ordinary income federally but is generally exempt from state and local income tax. If you live in a high tax state, that's a real edge for $SGOV. Check your 1099 and talk to a tax pro. Risk: how bumpy is the ride?

$JEPQ's risks: it's a stock fund first. When the Nasdaq-100 falls, $JEPQ falls with it, just usually less. You saw it this month: a 5.26% NAV drop in one day on November 20, 2026. The options cushion falls a little, but they don't protect you from a real bear market. And in strong up markets, the calls cap your upside. Over 3 years through October, $JEPQ did 20.04% a year versus 26.28% for the Nasdaq-100 Index, per J.P. Morgan. That gap is the price of the income.

$SGOV's risks: very few in the usual sense. It's U.S. government debt that matures in weeks. The real risks are quieter. If the Fed cuts rates, $SGOV's yield follows them down within months. And over long periods, T-bills often barely beat inflation, so your money is safe but not really growing.

That's why I don't see these as competitors. One is an engine, one is a parking spot.

$JEPI vs $SGOV and $QQQI vs $SGOV

People ask the same question about other option income funds, so here's the short version. $JEPI is $JEPQ's S&P 500 sibling: same 0.35% fee, a lower 12 month yield around 8.4% per StockAnalysis, and a beta of about 0.52 versus 0.84 for $JEPQ. It's the calmer of the two, but it's still a stock fund, not a T-bill substitute. $QQQI from NEOS is another Nasdaq-100 income fund, yielding about 16.6% per StockAnalysis with a 0.68% fee. Same logic applies to both: they're income from stocks, and $SGOV is income from Treasury bills.

How I'd actually use $JEPQ and $SGOV

I've been building dividend income for more than 10 years, and the way I'd frame this is by job, not by yield:

  • Money you need in the next 1 to 2 years (emergency fund, a house down payment, next year's living expenses): that's $SGOV territory. You don't want a 12 point swing in three weeks on money with a deadline.
  • Long-term income you won't touch for years: $JEPQ can make sense as part of an income portfolio, especially if you want Nasdaq exposure and monthly cash flow. Just size it so a bad month doesn't make you sell.
  • Waiting to invest: plenty of people park cash in $SGOV and move into stocks or income funds over time. That's a reasonable use of a T-bill fund.
  • Chasing the 12% headline: slow down. $JEPQ's yield isn't free money. It comes with stock risk, and the check changes every month.

What I personally look at with any option income fund: is the NAV holding up over time after the distributions are paid? On a total return basis, $JEPQ has done that well since 2022. November 2026 is a good reminder that it can still drop fast. The takeaway

On total return, $JEPQ has been far better: 20.04% a year over 3 years at NAV through October 31, 2026, versus 4.54% a year for $SGOV through September 30, 2026. It also pays about three times the income, near 12% versus about 3.6% to 3.9%. But $JEPQ is a Nasdaq stock fund with a payout that swings month to month, and it went from +8.37% to -3.50% for the year in just three weeks this November. $SGOV is T-bills: low return, nearly zero volatility and a steady check. Use $JEPQ for long-term income you can leave alone, and $SGOV for money you can't afford to see drop. Pick the one that matches the job.
